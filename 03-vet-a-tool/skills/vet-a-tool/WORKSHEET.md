# The Requirements Filter, on paper

The same check `/vet-a-tool` runs, for a pen and twenty minutes. Write your answers down before you open the vendor's website. If you read the marketing first, it becomes your requirements.

## 1. The job

Tool: ____________________  Job it would do: ____________________

Name the job, not just the tool. "Notetaker" and "notetaker on client financial calls" get different answers.

## 2. The data

Walk through one real example of what it would touch: ____________________

Circle the tier:

- **Tier 1, Confidential:** financials, legal documents, client personal details, payroll, contracts, passwords, anything under NDA
- **Tier 2, Internal:** processes, meeting notes, internal numbers, draft strategy
- **Tier 3, Shareable:** published content, marketing copy, public templates

If it falls between two tiers, circle the higher one, and write down why it moves.

## 3. Your lanes (circle every one that applies)

- **A:** financial advisory or RIA work, or you hold customer financial records
- **B:** you touch protected health information, including bookkeeping or admin for a healthcare client
- **C:** your organization runs Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace and IT controls it
- **D:** you serve several clients and each one's material is confidential
- **E:** none of the above, but you have made promises about people's data

E never means "no requirements." It means your requirements are the promises you made.

Requirements come from four places. Check each one:

- [ ] A regulator
- [ ] A contract you already signed (read your engagement letter for a third-party clause)
- [ ] A promise you published (your privacy policy, your website, what you told a client)
- [ ] A standard you chose yourself

## 5. The vendor's own words (fifteen minutes, timed)

| Question | Where to look | What it says (quote it) |
|---|---|---|
| Does it train on your data? Can you switch that off? | Terms of service | |
| Who does it share data with, by name? | Privacy policy, subprocessors page | |
| Will it notify you of a breach, in writing? | Privacy policy, Trust or Security page | |
| Where is data kept, and for how long? | Privacy policy | |
| (Lane B only) Will it sign a BAA on your plan? | Search "BAA" or "HIPAA" | |

The page labeled Privacy is not always the policy. Follow the links until you reach a dated document.

## Disqualifiers: any one ends it

- Trains on your data with no opt-out → never above Tier 3
- Cannot name who it shares with → never touches Tier 1
- No written breach commitment → never touches Tier 1
- Touches health information with no BAA → stop
- Fifteen minutes and no answer → a No for Tier 1

**Cannot tell** means: Tier 3, go ahead. Tier 2, go ahead with a written limit. Tier 1, No.
